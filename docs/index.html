Instant with spaceflick
Your keystrokes keep going to the space you left until the animation finishes. That's why it feels like more than half a second — because for your keyboard, it is.

The half-swipe is the point

The four-finger swipe is two things at once. While your fingers are down, the desktops track them one-to-one — that's the half-swipe you hold to see two spaces side by side. Then you lift, and the Dock plays a long ease-out before anything is actually focused.

Every other tool gets the speed by replacing the gesture: it swallows your swipe and posts a synthetic one at absurd velocity. That works, and it makes the interactive peek impossible — there is no finger to track any more.

spaceflick adjusts your real gesture instead. One passthrough event tap rewrites a single number as the swipe ends — the release velocity the Dock reads to decide how fast to finish. Nothing is intercepted, suppressed, or replayed. Swipe slowly and it behaves exactly as before.

Everything it does. Nothing it doesn't.

The gesture stays native

No suppression, no synthetic events. The finger-tracking phase never even sees the tap.

Half-swipe peek intact

Hold mid-swipe to see two spaces at once, exactly as macOS always let you.

Peek and let go still works

A slow release passes through untouched, so macOS still decides it from how far you dragged.

Safe at both ends

It won't fling you past the first or last space. At an edge it rubber-bands, like always.

~0% CPU

Two field reads and one field write per swipe. No timers, no polling. 10ms of CPU across 19 swipes.

SIP untouched

No scripting addition, no kext, no disabling System Integrity Protection. Just Accessibility.

“Synthetic-gesture tools” covers Blink and InstantSpaceSwitcher, which swallow the horizontal swipe and post their own. They do more than spaceflick does — hotkeys, menu bar, jump-to-space-N, wrap-around — so if you want those, use them. BetterTouchTool (paid) originated the technique and can do far more besides. yabai is a tiling window manager whose instant space switching needs SIP partially disabled.

Install. Grant. Done.

  1. Install and start it:

    brew install julienR2/tap/spaceflick && brew services start spaceflick

  2. Add /opt/homebrew/opt/spaceflick/bin/spaceflick to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. An event tap can't see the trackpad without it.

  3. Swipe. No restart needed — spaceflick waits for the grant and starts working the moment you flip the switch. It comes back at login and after a reboot.

Prefer to build it yourself, or want a proper /Applications app? Build from source. Free and open source under MIT — about 180 lines of Swift, no dependencies.

Thanks

spaceflick is a small idea on top of other people's reverse engineering. It exists because of these:

  • BetterTouchTool — where the velocity trick came from in the first place. Andreas Hegenberg worked out that the Dock's swipe events could be spoken to directly, years before anyone wrote it down.
  • darwin-fast-workspace-switch — reverse engineered BetterTouchTool to recover the private CGEventField numbers and published them.
  • InstantSpaceSwitcher — the compact C implementation that made the technique easy to read and confirmed the constants independently.
  • Blink — by far the biggest influence, and the reason this exists. Ben Koppe's app is where I first saw the trick working, and reading its source taught me the parts I'd have got wrong on my own: that the velocity sign already encodes direction, that the ordered space list has to be read per display, and that an edge guard is not optional. This site's shape is borrowed from its own, gratefully. Blink does much more than spaceflick — if you want hotkeys and jump-to-space, go get it.
  • yabai and CGSInternal — the long-running public record of what the private SkyLight and CoreGraphics space APIs actually are.

spaceflick's own contribution is narrow: apply the trick to the real gesture rather than a synthetic one, so the peek survives.
